Wangsheng Funeral Parlour · the ninth day of the plum month

Dear Traveler,it’s me again.

You have been walking a long time. I can tell — there is dust on your boots that isn’t from anywhere near here, and you have the particular look of someone who has said goodbye more often than hello this year.

Sit down. The tea is already cold, which means it has been waiting for you a while, which means I was right about when you’d arrive.

People come to my parlour expecting the worst hour of their lives. Sometimes it is. But I have learned that grief and joy use the same door, and that it is my job to make the room worth standing in either way. So I make things. Small things. A lamp for the nights that run long. A scarf for the walk home. A frame for the one blossom you kept.

None of it will bring anyone back. That is not what it’s for. It is for the part of you that keeps going, which — and I say this as a professional — is the more interesting part anyway.

Take something with you when you leave. Not because you need it. Because one day you will find it in a drawer and remember that somebody, somewhere, set out tea for you before you arrived.

Yours, until the last lantern —
Hu Tao

Things to take with you

Four keepsakes, made slowly, in small numbers. Each one arrives wrapped in paper with a handwritten card.

P.S.

I write one of these every season — a short letter, a note about what we’re making, and nothing else. No offers, no countdowns.
