A walk with her is never only a walk

It starts at the harbour gate at twenty to four with no plan, and finishes on the hill after sunset with a pocket full of things neither of us set out to collect. Scroll and the afternoon goes with you.

— written down afterwards, from memory, badly

Km 1 · the harbour gate

She sets off in the wrong direction on purpose

Two hundred metres the wrong way, so that we pass the noodle stall she likes and can claim it was on the route. There is a system to this. The system is: she does what she wants and narrates it as fate.

“Detours aren't detours if you never said where we were going.”

Km 2 · the long stair

Two hundred steps and a full running commentary

She counts them out loud, gets bored at forty, restarts at one, and by the top has convinced herself the stair is one hundred and eleven steps because that number is funnier. I checked later. It is two hundred and four.

“If you're out of breath you're doing it right. If I'm out of breath, you walked too fast.”

Km 4 · the field before the ridge

We stop because the light gets good

No other reason. She sits in the grass with her hat over her face, says she is thinking about work, and is very obviously asleep for eleven minutes. I write three lines in a notebook and get them wrong.

“Wake me at gold. Not amber, not pink. Gold.”

Km 6 · the top, after sunset

She stops talking for the last two hundred metres

Which is the only genuinely surprising thing that happens all day. At the top she sits on the wall, swings her legs, and says the one sentence she has clearly been saving since the harbour gate.

“See? Nothing happened. That's the whole point of a good day — you can't tell anyone about it afterwards.”
