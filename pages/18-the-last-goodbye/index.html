The last goodbye should be allowed to be small

We make four objects for the end of something. Not grand, not loud, not designed to be photographed. Just things solid enough to hold while you say it.

Six memories first

i.

You were late, and she waited anyway

Forty minutes past the arranged time, in the rain, under an awning that leaked in exactly one place — the place she was standing. She did not mention it once. She mentioned it every single day for the next four years.

“I wasn't waiting. I was standing. Different thing entirely.”

ii.

The tea was always too strong

You said so the first time and then never again, because the third time you noticed she had started leaving the pot to stand for a minute less. Neither of you acknowledged it. That was the whole friendship, really, in one pot of tea.

“It's supposed to hurt slightly. That's how you know you're awake.”

iii.

She talked about endings constantly

At dinner, on walks, in the middle of other people's good news. It was never morbid; it was the opposite. She was the only person you knew who treated an ending as a thing you could plan well, like a meal, or a route home.

“Everyone gets one. Might as well make yours good.”

iv.

There was a song she hummed and never named

Four notes, always the same four, always slightly off. You looked for it for years afterwards and never found it, which is probably correct. Some things are meant to only exist in one throat.

“It's not a real song. I made it up when I was nine and I refuse to improve it.”

v.

The last time was an ordinary Tuesday

No speeches. She was halfway through a sentence about a delivery of paper and got distracted by a bird. You left first. You said, “see you next week,” and you meant it, and that is the kindest possible way for it to have happened.

“Go on. It's cold and you're hovering.”

vi.

You still set out two cups

Not every day. Not sadly. Just sometimes, on a Tuesday, out of a habit your hands kept when the rest of you moved on. This is not a problem to be fixed. It is just the shape a person leaves behind.

“Keep the habit. Lose the guilt. Those are separable.”
