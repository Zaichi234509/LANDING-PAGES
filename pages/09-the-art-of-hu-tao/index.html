Room three · west wing

The Art of Hu Tao: six works, one room

This room gathers six works made between 1904 and last spring by five artists who never met, and who share only a subject and a persistent difficulty: nobody has ever managed to paint her holding still.

Four of the six were painted from memory. The fifth was painted from life and took eleven sittings, of which she attended three. The sixth is a corridor with nobody in it, and is generally agreed to be the best likeness in the collection.

Plate 01 — Director, Seated

Oil on linen · 1904 · artist unrecorded

The only known work painted from life. Eleven sittings were arranged; she attended three, and on the third reportedly moved the chair "to somewhere with a better view of the door".

Plate 02 — Two Cups, One Cold

Oil on board · 1948 · Wen Qiu

Painted after a single visit to the parlour. The second cup is untouched; the artist insisted for forty years that this was an accident of composition and not a statement.

Plate 03 — Study of Hands

Charcoal and chalk on laid paper · 1971 · anon.

One of nineteen surviving studies from the same sketchbook. The object in the hands is unidentified; the Archive's position is that it is a plum blossom charm, and the Archive is aware this is a guess.

Plate 04 — Study of a Lantern

Oil on panel · 1996 · Ito Kanae

Painted over four nights in a corridor with the lights off. The artist's stated aim was to record "the exact amount of orange a room needs before it stops being frightening".

Abandoned at the halfway line and hung anyway, at the artist's request. The Archive receives four letters a year asking us to have it completed. We do not intend to.

Plate 06 — The Long Corridor

Oil on linen · 2024 · Zhou Yan

No figure appears in this work. It is, by common agreement among everyone who knew the subject, the most accurate portrait in the room. Photography is not permitted.

Curator's note

On the difficulty of the subject

Every artist represented here failed in the same way. Asked to paint a person who talks continuously, moves constantly and treats a portrait sitting as a social occasion, each of them ended up painting the room instead — the chair, the cup, the corridor, the light she had recently walked out of.

The Archive has stopped regarding this as failure. Six attempts, five artists, one hundred and twenty years, and the collection has arrived at something more useful than a likeness: a record of the shape a person leaves in a space.

— K. Almeida, Keeper of Room Three
